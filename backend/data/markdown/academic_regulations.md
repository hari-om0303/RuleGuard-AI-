# Northbridge Institute of Technology — Academic Regulations Manual (2025-2026 Edition)

## Document Information
- **Document Code**: NIT-REG-2025-v1
- **Issuing Authority**: Office of the Dean of Academic Affairs & Academic Senate
- **Scope**: Applicable to all enrolled Undergraduate (B.Tech, B.S.) and Postgraduate (M.Tech, M.S., Ph.D.) students across all academic departments of Northbridge Institute of Technology.

---

## Section 1.0 General Provisions & Admission Regulations

### Section 1.1 Scope and Authority of Academic Regulations
The regulations set forth in this manual govern all academic, administrative, and instructional activities conducted at Northbridge Institute of Technology (hereinafter referred to as "NIT" or "the Institute"). Enrolled students, faculty members, academic administrators, and examination coordinators are bound by the provisions outlined herein. The Academic Council of NIT reserves the sole authority to interpret, amend, or temporarily suspend any provision within this document. Any student admitted to any degree or diploma program at NIT is deemed to have accepted and agreed to comply with all rules and regulations established by the Institute.

### Section 1.2 Academic Calendar & Semester Structure
The academic session of NIT is divided into two regular semester sessions—the Fall Semester (starting in August and ending in December) and the Spring Semester (starting in January and ending in May)—plus an optional Summer Term (conducted between June and July). Each regular semester comprises a minimum of fifteen (15) full instructional weeks, excluding examination periods, registration days, and official institute holidays. The Summer Term comprises seven (7) intensive instructional weeks dedicated primarily to backlog clearance, remedial coursework, and approved industrial internships.

### Section 1.3 Medium of Instruction & Official Communication
The primary medium of instruction, evaluation, examination, and official correspondence across all academic programs at NIT is English. All official notifications, academic warnings, fee notices, examination schedules, and committee decisions will be communicated to students exclusively through their official NIT institutional email addresses (`@northbridge.edu`). Students are required to check their institutional email inbox daily and are held responsible for adhering to all published deadlines and notices.

### Section 1.4 Advanced Standing & Credit Equivalency Protocol
Candidates admitted with advanced standing, such as diploma holders entering through lateral entry into the second year of B.Tech programs, will be awarded block credit transfers based on evaluation by the Departmental Equivalency Committee. Transferred credits earned at accredited external diploma institutions are recorded as 'CR' (Credit Transferred) on the academic transcript. Transferred credits do not carry quality grade points and are excluded from SGPA and CGPA computations, though they count fully toward satisfying total graduation credit requirements.

## Section 2.0 Registration, Course Load & Credit Framework

### Section 2.1 Degree Credit Requirements
To qualify for the award of the Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech) degree at Northbridge Institute of Technology, an undergraduate student must successfully complete and earn a minimum total of one hundred and sixty (160) academic credits. These credits are distributed across General Engineering Core Courses (40 credits), Departmental Specialization Core Courses (60 credits), Professional Electives (24 credits), Open Electives (16 credits), Laboratory & Workshop Practice (12 credits), and the Senior Year Capstone Project (8 credits). For Postgraduate (M.Tech) degrees, a minimum of sixty-four (64) academic credits is required for graduation.

### Section 2.2 Semester Registration & Enrollment Procedures
Every student must formally register for their intended courses prior to the commencement of each semester on the official dates specified in the approved Academic Calendar. Registration involves selecting courses, obtaining advisor approval, verifying prerequisite compliance, and clearing all outstanding financial liabilities with the Accounts Division. A student who fails to complete formal course registration by the published registration deadline will not be permitted to attend classes, access online learning portals, or participate in campus laboratory exercises.

### Section 2.3 Course Load Constraints & Credit Limits
In any regular semester (Fall or Spring), a full-time undergraduate student is permitted to register for a minimum of sixteen (16) credits and a maximum of twenty-six (26) credits. Registration for fewer than 16 credits requires prior written authorization from the Dean of Academic Affairs and is permitted only for final-semester students completing remaining credit deficits. Registration for up to 28 credits is permitted only for high-performing students who have maintained a Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of 8.50 or higher in the immediately preceding academic year.

### Section 2.4 Prerequisite Compliance and Course Sequencing
Courses offered by NIT departments may specify prerequisite or co-requisite courses to ensure adequate foundational preparation. A student is ineligible to enroll in a course if they have not obtained a passing grade ('D' or higher) in all prerequisite courses. If a student attempts to register for a course without satisfying prerequisites through administrative error, the Registrar's Office will automatically cancel the registration without refund after the add/drop period.

### Section 2.5 Major & Minor Degree Specialization Track
Undergraduate B.Tech students maintaining a CGPA of 7.50 or higher at the conclusion of their second semester may apply to pursue an additional Minor Specialization Track comprising twenty (20) additional credits in a secondary discipline (such as Data Science, Artificial Intelligence, Financial Technology, or Robotics). Minor specialization courses are taken over Semesters 3 through 8 in addition to standard degree requirements.

## Section 3.0 Grading System, CGPA Calculation & Semester Promotion

### Section 3.1 Letter Grades and Quality Grade Points
Academic performance in each course is evaluated and recorded using a letter grading scale. Each letter grade corresponds to specific quality grade points per credit hour as detailed below:
- Grade 'S' (Outstanding): 10.0 Grade Points (Awarded for top 5% performance exceeding 90% marks)
- Grade 'A' (Excellent): 9.0 Grade Points (Awarded for 80% - 89% marks)
- Grade 'B' (Very Good): 8.0 Grade Points (Awarded for 70% - 79% marks)
- Grade 'C' (Good): 7.0 Grade Points (Awarded for 60% - 69% marks)
- Grade 'D' (Satisfactory / Pass): 6.0 Grade Points (Awarded for 50% - 59% marks)
- Grade 'E' (Marginal Pass): 4.0 Grade Points (Awarded for 40% - 49% marks)
- Grade 'F' (Fail): 0.0 Grade Points (Awarded for performance below 40% marks)
- Grade 'I' (Incomplete): 0.0 Grade Points (Temporary grade reserved for documented emergencies)
- Grade 'W' (Withdrawn): Neutral (Does not impact CGPA calculation)

### Section 3.2 Graduation CGPA Threshold
To qualify for the award of an Undergraduate Bachelor of Technology degree, a student must achieve a minimum overall Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of 5.00 on a 10.00 scale across all credits completed. A student who completes all required course credits but finishes with a CGPA between 4.50 and 4.99 will be granted one extension semester to repeat up to three courses to improve their CGPA to the mandatory 5.00 threshold.

### Section 3.3 Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) & CGPA Formulas
The Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) is calculated at the end of each semester by taking the sum of the products of credits assigned to each course and the grade points earned in that course, divided by the total number of credits registered in that semester.
$$\text{SGPA} = \frac{\sum (C_i \times G_i)}{\sum C_i}$$
The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is calculated similarly across all courses completed from the student's initial enrollment date to the evaluation date. Grades earned in repeated courses replace the previous 'F' grade in CGPA calculations, although the original 'F' grade remains visible on the transcript.

### Section 3.4 Academic Probation & Disqualification
An undergraduate student whose Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) falls below 4.50 in any regular semester will automatically be placed on Academic Probation for the subsequent semester. A student on Academic Probation is restricted to a maximum course load of 18 credits and must attend mandatory academic counseling sessions organized by the Student Success Center. If a student remains on Academic Probation for two consecutive semesters, their academic case will be escalated to the Standing Academic Review Board, which may recommend academic suspension or permanent expulsion from the Institute.

### Section 3.5 Honors Classification & Class Rank Determination
Graduating B.Tech students who complete their degree in eight (8) consecutive regular semesters with a final CGPA of 8.50 or higher and zero backlog courses throughout their enrollment are awarded First Class with Distinction (Honors). Students graduating with a CGPA between 7.00 and 8.49 receive First Class classification, while those graduating with a CGPA between 5.00 and 6.99 receive Second Class classification.

## Section 4.0 Attendance Regulations & Shortage Rules

### Section 4.1 Mandatory Class & Laboratory Attendance
Regular and punctual class attendance is considered an essential component of the educational process at Northbridge Institute of Technology. Students are expected to attend all scheduled lectures, tutorials, laboratory practicals, field trips, and seminar sessions for every course in which they are registered. Faculty members record attendance digitally via the NIT Academic Portal at the conclusion of each class session.

### Section 4.2 Minimum Attendance Requirement for Semester Examinations
Students must maintain a minimum of 75% attendance in each registered course to be eligible to sit for the end-semester examinations. No exceptions or waivers are granted below this threshold under standard academic regulations. A student whose attendance in a course falls below 75% at the end of the instructional semester will be awarded a grade of 'FA' (Failed due to Attendance Shortage) for that course and will be barred from appearing in the end-semester examination.

### Section 4.3 Attendance Shortage Notification Protocol
Course instructors are required to publish mid-semester attendance status updates on the Academic Portal at the end of Week 4 and Week 9 of the semester. Students whose attendance falls below 80% at these checkpoints will receive an automated warning alert via institutional email. However, the ultimate responsibility for tracking and maintaining requisite attendance lies entirely with the student.

## Section 5.0 Continuous Internal Evaluation & Term Work

### Section 5.1 Evaluation Component Weightage
The overall grade evaluation for every standard theory course at NIT is split into two main components: Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) carrying 50% total weightage, and End Semester Examination (ESE) carrying 50% total weightage. Continuous Internal Evaluation comprises mid-semester examinations (25%), written quizzes and homework assignments (15%), and class participation/mini-projects (10%).

### Section 5.2 Mid-Semester Examinations and Missed Tests
Mid-semester examinations are conducted centrally during Week 8 of the regular semester. Attendance in mid-semester exams is mandatory. If a student misses a mid-semester exam due to verified institutional deputation (such as representing NIT in national sports or academic competitions), a makeup mid-semester exam will be scheduled by the department within ten (10) working days. No makeup mid-semester exams are permitted for unexcused personal absences.

## Section 6.0 Course Drop, Add & Withdrawal Rules

### Section 6.1 Course Add/Drop Period
During the first ten (10) calendar days of each regular semester (known as the Add/Drop Period), a student may modify their course schedule by adding new courses or dropping registered courses without academic penalty or notation on their permanent academic transcript. All Add/Drop requests must be submitted electronically through the Academic Portal and require digital approval from the assigned Academic Advisor.

### Section 6.2 Course Drop Refund Rules
Courses dropped during the official Add/Drop Period will entitle the student to a 100% credit adjustment toward tuition fees for that semester. Any course dropped after the expiration of the ten-day Add/Drop Period will not qualify for any tuition fee refund or credit adjustment.

### Section 6.3 Course Withdrawal Deadline
A student may formally withdraw from a registered course without academic penalty up to the end of Week 8 of the active semester upon written recommendation of the Academic Advisor. A course from which a student formally withdraws will be recorded on the permanent academic transcript with a grade notation of 'W'. The grade 'W' carries no quality grade points and does not affect the calculation of SGPA or CGPA. A student is not permitted to withdraw from a course if doing so reduces their active semester credit load below sixteen (16) credits.
